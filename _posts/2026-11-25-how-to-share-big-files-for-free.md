## 4 Ways to Share Big Files Online (Compared)

### 1. Email attachments
Email is the first thing most people try, and the first thing to fail. Most providers cap attachments at roughly 20 to 25MB, so a single photo album or video clip is often too big.
- **Best for:** small documents.
- **Problem:** large files bounce back with a "file too large" message.

### 2. Cloud storage links (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive)
You upload the file, then share a link. This works for big files, but you pay for it in time and storage.
- **Best for:** files you want to keep online long term.
- **Problem:** free plans have limited storage, the upload has to finish before anyone can download, and most services want you to sign in.

### 3. Free file transfer websites
Services built for sending files, such as WeTransfer, let you share without much setup. Free plans usually come with a size cap per transfer and a link that expires.
- **Best for:** medium-sized files when you don't mind the cap.
- **Problem:** the cap, plus your file sits on someone else's server while the link is active.

### 4. Peer-to-peer sharing (FileDropShare)
With peer-to-peer (P2P) sharing, your file is never uploaded to a server. It moves directly between the two devices over an encrypted connection.
- **Best for:** very large files, private files, and quick one-off transfers.
- **No limits to hit:** nothing is stored on a server, so there is no storage quota to run out of.
- **Private:** no copy of your file is kept online.
- **Fast start:** the download begins as soon as the receiver opens the link.

## Why Peer-to-Peer Is the Easiest Way to Share Big Files for Free

Most "free" file sharing costs the provider money to store and serve your file, which is why they add size caps, expiring links and sign-up walls. P2P skips that step entirely. There is no file on a server, so there is nothing to cap.

## How to Share a Zip File or a Folder

FileDropShare sends files, so to share a whole folder, compress it first:
- **Windows:** right-click the folder, choose *Send to* then *Compressed (zipped) folder*.
- **Mac:** right-click the folder and choose *Compress*.

Then add the zip file and share the link like any other file. Zip files of any size work the same way.

## Tips for Sending Big Files Smoothly

- **Use a stable connection.** Ethernet or strong Wi-Fi on both sides makes large transfers noticeably faster.
- **Keep both devices awake.** If a laptop goes to sleep, the transfer pauses.
- **Check free disk space.** The receiver needs room for the full file.
